# 10.19 progress overview

## Paddle hits: a finder built on the ball's path doesn't work on new play

- **What was tested:** a hit finder that flags any sharp turn or sudden speed-up in the tracked ball (not only
  the bounce-like dips the bounce finder looks for). The rules were written down using minute 1 of the
  operator's hit marks, then tested once on minute 2, which had been kept back.
- **Result:** on minute 1 it found 7 of 22 hits. On minute 2 it found **2 of 22**, and only 2 of its 27 boxes
  were hits. Failed the bar set in advance.
- **Why:** the ball's path is broken exactly where the paddle meets the ball. Near the camera the ball is
  almost never seen at contact (0 of 9 near hits usable in minute 2). On the far side the tracker jumps or
  loses it, and most boxes land on wobble in the track rather than on hits.
- **Decision (ADR-156):** stop tuning rules on the ball's path for hits. Hits come back only with a better
  ball track near the players, or with a cue that doesn't need the ball (where the wrist is, when the arm swings).
- **Kept:** all 44 hit marks (`eval/labels/hits/`), the test set for whichever of those comes next.

## Paddle hits: the arm swing works where the ball doesn't

- **What was tested:** instead of the ball, watch the players' wrists. A hit is a fast arm movement, and the
  pose model sees the arm even when the ball is blurred or hidden. Rules written down on minute 1, tested
  once on minute 2.
- **Result:** found **12 of 22 hits** on minute 2, including **4 of 9 near-side hits** (the ball-based finder
  got 0 of those). About 1 in 4 swings it flags is a real hit; random timing would find about 3 hits.
  Passed every check set in advance, but only just.
- **What it means:** it's the first workable route to counting hits, especially near the camera. It isn't
  a hit counter yet: most swings it flags are players moving without hitting.
- **Next:** combine the swing with the ball (a swing when the ball is close to that player), and check on a
  third minute or another court before relying on it.

## Paddle hits: arm swing + ball check passes on a fresh minute

- **What was tested:** the arm-swing finder, keeping a swing only when the ball is in play and isn't clearly
  somewhere else. Rules fixed before the operator marked minute 3, then tested once on it.
- **Result on minute 3 (27 hits):** found **18** (near side **7 of 12**), and **more than half** of what it
  flagged were real hits (18 of 32). Random timing would find about 2. Passed all five checks set in advance.
  The plain swing finder also held up a second time (19 of 27).
- **What it means:** we now have a working hit finder on this court: about 2 in 3 hits found, about 1 in 2
  flags right. Good enough to start building hit counts and shot timelines, with visible error; not a referee.
- **Decision (ADR-157):** hit work builds on this finder from now on.
- **Next:** check it on another court (the glare court) and with the production detections before relying on it.

## Paddle hits: does it still work with the real pipeline's ball detector?

- Re-ran the same hit finder with the production ball detector (it was built and tested on a sharper research
  detector). Result: still works, but weaker — finds **51% of hits instead of 63%**; the share of flags that
  are real hits is unchanged (~45%).
- Reason: the production detector loses the ball more often, so the "ball is in play" check passes less often.
  The accuracy of what it flags is fine; it's coverage that follows the detector.
- Takeaway: the finder is real on the production stack too, just lower-recall. Before shipping a hit count,
  either accept ~half the hits or improve the ball track (or run the sharper detector in the pipeline).

## Paddle hits: works on the second court too (the glare one)

- Ran the exact same finder, untouched, on a different camera and the harder glare court (operator marked
  29 hits in two minutes).
- Result: found **16 of 29 (55%)**, half the near-side hits, a third of its flags were real, far above chance.
  Passed every check set in advance.
- This is the result that matters: the finder isn't a fluke of the one clean court. It holds on a second
  court and with the production detector (just lower recall).
- Both follow-up checks from ADR-157 are now closed. Remaining unknown: a different venue (different lighting
  and camera), which we have no footage for yet.

## Paddle hits: NOT production-ready (operator call, 2026-10-19)

- Watching the detections on the synced video+map, the operator judged hits **currently unusable in production**.
  Over the two marked minutes: 39% precise (27 of 70 flags real), 61% recall — too many false "hits" to show a
  venue as a shot count or shot-by-shot stat.
- This is a **detector-level** block, not a rule gap (see the 2026-10-19 accuracy-levers entry): the false flags
  are near-side ball-unseen swings, the ball is lost at contact, and rule-tuning is exhausted. Moving it needs
  better contact-time ball tracking or a learned classifier with far more labels — both large.
- Production-grade signals from the same pipeline remain: rally cutting (shipped) and player positions/movement
  (floor points, reliable). Hits are parked as a research result (ADR-157) until a detector improvement.
- Tooling kept: synced video + 2D signal map (`scratchpad/hitdev/court_signals.html`, served :8451) showing
  confirmed/false/missed hits against the operator marks; the 71 hit labels in `eval/labels/hits/`.

## Do the new signals make better reels? Swing activity yes, movement no

- Tested whether the new pose signals help *pick* highlight rallies (the actual product), scored against the
  hand quality grades on brickwall (49 rallies). This needs only rank-correlation, not the per-hit accuracy
  that made hits unusable as a counter.
- **Swing activity helps:** adds ~+0.08 AUC on top of rally length for spotting highlight-worthy rallies —
  a real, if modest, lead. So the swing detector earns its keep as a reel-ranking feature even though it
  can't count hits.
- **Player movement does not:** barely separates good from ordinary rallies, adds nothing over length. The
  "scrambling = exciting" idea didn't hold here.
- Status: a lead, not a ranker change — the grade rubric itself was never consistency-checked, and it's one
  video. Next if pursued: validate swing_rate on a second graded video before wiring it into src/select.py.

## Reel-ranking signals: the brickwall lead did not replicate

- Re-ran the highlight-ranking test on a second graded video (pb_draft_cup). The swing-activity lead from
  brickwall **did not hold** — swing helped on one video and hurt on the other; movement did the reverse.
- Honest conclusion: neither new signal reliably beats rally length for picking highlights. The brickwall
  result was overfitting to one small, never-consistency-checked grade set.
- Not wiring either into the ranker. Rally **duration** stays the dependable signal. Revisit only if the
  highlight-grade ground truth is made consistent / larger.

## E2E: the reel pipeline with the Jev guardrail wired in

- There was no integrated pipeline yet (Jev was standalone scripts). Built the guardrail into the selection
  stage and ran the real pipeline end to end on 14 sessions, using the Jev scores already saved (so no footage
  was sent anywhere).
- **Result:** the guardrail lifts the reel's real-clip share from 66% -> 71% (75% -> 83% once serve-only clips
  are set aside), removing 6 junk clips for the cost of 1 real one. One session that was all practice got its
  top picks cleaned out.
- It's a safe, modest clean-up, exactly as the earlier Jev tests predicted. Leftover junk is mostly warm-ups,
  which Jev can't tell from a real point in still frames.
- Dry run only (saved scores). A live end-to-end run calls Jev on fresh footage = operator-run (sending PGC
  footage to TypeSafe is blocked for the agent). The live code path is built but not yet exercised.

## Highlight types: only "long rally" is consistent across both videos

- Tried to produce every feasible + consistent highlight type (long rally, kitchen war, fast dink), with the
  rule that a type must separate highlight-worthy rallies on BOTH graded videos (not one).
- Result: only **long rally** passed. Kitchen war and fast dink looked great on brickwall (0.70, 0.81) but
  collapsed on pb_draft_cup (0.55, 0.48) -- the same one-video mirage as the earlier swing lead. The
  both-videos rule caught it before anything shipped.
- Caveat: the test scores "does the type predict a highlight grade", which isn't the same as "does it detect
  the type". Kitchen war / fast dink may still be valid *detectors*; proving it needs typed labels across both
  courts (we only have highlight 1/2 grades, soft and never double-checked), or a cross-court clip review.
- Produced per-rally type tags for both videos (cache/highlight_types/) to eyeball. Shippable type today:
  long rally. The blocker for the rest is, again, the ground truth.
