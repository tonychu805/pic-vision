# 2026-08-22 — Player-position/motion signal explored as a new angle on PIC-31, committee review of the session's work

Picked up from 2026-08-21's TrackNetV3/kitchen-dinks thread. The operator asked to revisit player geometry as a rally-boundary signal (a superseded direction, `ADR-028`/`ADR-047`), reopened because the crossing-count signal's known failure modes (`PIC-31`, `PIC-34`, and 08-21's kitchen-dinks finding) all share one root cause: everything tried so far is derived from the ball-crossing signal itself. Player motion/position is genuinely independent of that signal.

## Pose detection sanity check — before training anything, check the cheap thing first

Ran off-the-shelf `yolov8n-pose`/`yolov8x-pose` (zero-shot, no fine-tuning) on four `brickwall_30fps` frames. Near-team keypoints are clean and reliable (12–17/17, conf 0.69–0.95); the far team is never detected, at either model size, even with the confidence threshold dropped to surface marginal hits — visual inspection confirmed this is net/signage occlusion specific to this camera's framing, not a general too-small-to-see problem. A frame caught right after a rally end showed an unmistakable posture contrast (crouched ready-stance vs. arms-out walking-in-for-a-fist-bump) — pose is a real, cheap signal for the near court specifically.

## Near-team pre-serve stillness — a real, sharp signal, with a self-caught measurement mistake along the way

First comparison (pre-serve speed vs. mean mid-rally speed) came back inconsistent and looked like a failed hypothesis — until the actual time-series plot showed mid-rally speed isn't uniformly high (kitchen dinks keep feet planted too). Corrected to compare against the *preceding dead-time* baseline instead: near-team ankle speed drops to **7–17% of the surrounding dead-time level** in the last second before serve, across all 3 checkable rally boundaries — sharp and consistent, not a subtle trend. Also surfaced, unprompted: dead time is the *noisiest* part of the signal (not the quiet part), and rally end shows the opposite pattern — a motion spike, not a dip.

**The fusion idea this points toward, not yet tested:** stillness-dip → arm a candidate → require a net-crossing burst within some window after → confirm. Aimed directly at `PIC-31` (crossing bursts alone can't separate a real rally from a courtesy tap-back). The real test — checking this against `PIC-37`'s already-known dead-time-crossing false positives — hasn't been run yet.

## Baseline-vs-kitchen-line position, and a timing signal that fell out of testing it

The operator proposed a sharper, positional version of the same idea: offense stands at the baseline, still; defense is either both-baseline or one-at-kitchen-line. Testing it in three widening passes turned up more than expected:

- **Tight pre-serve window:** a clean binary split — rallies 1/4 at the baseline (−1.8ft), rallies 2/3 at the kitchen line (14.6–14.8ft, right at the calibrated 15ft NVZ line). Rally 1 independently confirmed by frame (ball visibly in hand at the baseline).
- **Widened window (6s before serve):** didn't find a second player — found the *same* player walking from baseline to kitchen line over the preceding several seconds. This directly tested the operator's read that a returner should be planted at the near baseline in rallies 2/3, and didn't confirm it: no baseline presence was found there at all, near or far, in 6 full seconds. Left genuinely open — could be the label's "start" timestamp landing after the actual serve, the returner being outside this camera's frame, or the near team serving in 2/3 too.
- **Post-serve window (13s after serve):** the real payoff. Rallies 1/4 (serving, confirmed/matched) hold the baseline 3.4–6.9s *after* the point starts before advancing. Rallies 2/3's second near-team player is never seen before serve at all, first appears near the baseline 1.9–2.5s in, and reaches the net in under 2s — a distinctly different tempo. That's a second, independent piece of corroborating evidence for the operator's serve-direction read, though still short of certainty (needs real playback of the serve motion, not more tracking data, to fully settle).
