# 09.24 progress overview

## Rehearsal of sending a game in 10-minute parts: it works, and it found one real bug (fixed)

A full test ran against the real cloud, using a stand-in camera that played real Court 4 footage in a loop, a separate test venue and 10-minute parts:

- **Parts went out while recording.** Each 10-minute piece uploaded in under 2 minutes on this connection.
- **After Stop, the last part's reels were ready in about 10 minutes.**
- **One link for the whole game:** the reels from the parts landed together on the same share page.

**The bug:** a 10-minute stretch with almost no play made the job **fail** instead of saying "no rallies". It was always possible, but rare when a whole session was sent at once. With 10-minute parts it'll happen at every break. Now such a stretch finishes normally with "no rallies found", and the rest of the game's reels are unaffected. Confirmed on the rehearsal's own footage.

**Worth knowing for the weekend:**
- **Venue upload speed** is still the big unknown. This office's fast mode keeps up easily; its slow mode (seen before) wouldn't. Check it with the Diagnostics tab at the venue.
- **One court is fine.** Several courts all sending 10-minute parts at once would start to queue: each part takes ~15–17 minutes of cloud time, and 4 run at a time.
- **The venue computer still needs the new desktop release** for the one-link-per-game part.

## Each reel job now records which GPU machine ran it

The rehearsal ran the same 10 minutes of footage at 36 fps on one rented machine and 74 fps on another, with no record of which card each was. Every reel job now saves its GPU type and RunPod machine ID, so a slow job can be traced to its hardware and matched to its billing line. Live and tested against the real console (live test L10). Changing which cards we prefer is **on hold** (operator, 09.24) until there's data.

## Two cameras at once: tested, works

A second rehearsal recorded two stand-in cameras at the same time, each sending 10-minute parts:

- **All 6 parts finished.**
- **Each game got its own link, with nothing mixed between them.**
- **The last reels were ready 8–10 minutes after Stop.**
- **The no-rallies fix worked in the real cloud:** the dead stretch that crashed yesterday now finishes as "no rallies found".
- **Load:** at most 3 cloud jobs ran at once, under the limit of 4.

**The main risk found:** rented machines vary a lot. Two machines took about 6 minutes just to start, where most take 1–3. The same card type ran twice as fast on one machine as on another. On a bad draw, that can add ~5 minutes to the wait after Stop. Keeping one machine running for the whole game (planned for after the weekend) removes most of it.

## Reusing the GPU machine for a game's next part: built, live, tested

Renting and starting a GPU machine takes 2–6 minutes per part. Now a machine that finishes a part of a game waits up to 12 minutes for that game's next part instead of shutting down. A real test showed:
- the waiting machine took part 3 straight away, with no rental or startup;
- a second machine that would have been a duplicate waiter shut itself down;
- the last idle machine shut itself down after its 12 minutes;
- all four parts landed on one link;
- nothing was left running or billing.

A part never waits for a busy machine: it gets a fresh machine as before. With 10-minute parts and the cheaper cards, the waiting machine is often still busy when the next part arrives, so it saves some rentals rather than every one.

**Skipping the convert step: built, left off** (operator, 09.24). It saves up to 2 minutes per part and showed no accuracy cost against 37 hand-labelled rallies. It does change which borderline rallies are found, though, so the weekend stays on the tested setup.

**Netlify credits at 50%** for the cycle (09-20 to 10-19). The cause: 32 production deploys this week (28 console, 4 share page), each costing credits. From now on, console changes get batched into fewer pushes, with none before the weekend unless something is broken.

## Back-to-back bookings stopped the new recording (found and fixed live)

The first bookings to start by themselves (Office, Tournament 3 and 4, 04:00 UTC) worked. But at 05:00 the next booking on the same cameras began in the same minute as the previous one ended. The console sent the "start" and "stop" together, the Office computer ran the start first, and the old booking's stop then **killed the new recording 6 seconds in**. With sending in parts on, those 6-second stubs were also sent to the cloud and failed there.

**Fixed in the console's booking timer** (a database change, live, no deploy): stops now always go out before starts. Checked in a local database copy against this exact 05:00 case. Not fixed yet: the desktop still treats "stop" as "stop whatever this camera is recording". The full fix, where a stop only ends the recording it belongs to, needs a desktop release; after the weekend.

Also learned: **a Stop pressed by hand doesn't send the recording unless sending in parts is on.** Only a booking reaching its end sends automatically. For the weekend, keep sending in parts on (Settings, reached via the "Scan options" button on the Cameras page).

## A booking's stop now only ends its own recording (desktop 1.6.8)

Follow-up to the 05:00 back-to-back bug above, fixed properly:
- **Desktop 1.6.8:** a booking's stop names the recording it belongs to, and the desktop only stops that one. A recording belonging to a different game keeps running, and the log says "Kept recording". A recording with no saved session is stopped as before, so nothing can run on forever.
- **Console (live):** pressing Start during a booking attaches the recording to that booking. So a game restarted by hand still ends when its booking does, instead of running (and sending parts) until someone notices.
- **Console (live):** pushes that change nothing the website runs (Cloudflare code, tests, database files, docs) no longer trigger a paid Netlify deploy.

Tested: desktop 248 tests, console 199 + 18 live, the booking timer 6 checks in a local database. The first 1.6.8 build failed on an unrelated timing test (`camera-sync.test.js`, "a burst of changes collapses into one heartbeat"), which splits into two heartbeats on a slow build machine. A re-run passed. That test is flaky and should be made less timing-dependent.

**Install 1.6.8 on the Office computer when nothing is recording.** Quitting the app stops recordings.

## Two cloud failures seen in the live Office session, both fixed

In the 07:00–08:00 Office session, both courts' part 5 lost ~17 minutes:
1. **Docker Hub refused to hand out our machine software.** Its free limit for anonymous downloads is shared with every other RunPod customer on the same building, and it ran out. RunPod stopped the two machines. **Fixed:** machines now download logged in to Docker Hub (a read-only token for your account, saved in RunPod), with a limit of 200 per hour of our own. Tested on a real machine first.
2. **Our system didn't notice the stopped machines** and waited 8 minutes for them. Then, 15 minutes without progress made the part look abandoned, so it was picked up a second time. **Fixed:** a machine RunPod stops is removed and retried within about a minute, and a part is only treated as abandoned after 30 minutes with no progress (a working machine reports every 20 seconds).

Also seen: at 08:20, **none of our five cheaper cards was available anywhere on RunPod.** **Fixed:** the three pricier cards (RTX 4090 $0.74/hr, RTX 5000/6000 Ada ~$0.84/hr) are now tried as a last resort, only when all five cheaper ones are taken, so a part costs a few cents more instead of failing.

Tested: job runner 41 unit + 11 component tests (including a replay of the stopped-machine case), database checks locally, Python 243. Live. None of this needed a Netlify deploy.
