# 10.18 progress overview

## Court map with bounce points: zones right, but most bounces missing and many false dots

- Operator question after the showcase page: can we draw a 2D court map of where the ball bounces? Ball
  flight stays off the map (one camera can't place a ball in the air on the floor).
- Coarse test (kitchen or deep, per side), pre-registered: when the system finds a bounce, it puts it in the
  right zone 51 of 52 times. **Passes**, but that was nearly guaranteed by how "found" is defined.
- The real limits are unchanged: it finds about 1 in 3 bounces (near side 23%, far side 43%), and about
  4 in 10 of its bounce picks are not bounces at all. On a map those would be dots with nothing behind them.
- Measured on the sharper tiled detection pass, not the production one, on two cameras at PGC.
- **Next if pursued:** have the operator judge each dot on a demo map from playback (are the dots real?).
  That, not zone accuracy, decides whether a bounce map is shippable.

## Hand points: one of the two tools lands on the hand

- Operator judged 76 players (the click page). The whole-body tool (RTMPose) put its hand points on the hand
  for 31 of 33 near players and all 36 far players: passes the 70% bar set in advance.
  The hands-only tool (MediaPipe) mostly found nothing: fails.
- **Weaker than it looks:** the whole-body tool always draws all its points, so the review wasn't blind;
  and on far players a hand is ~10 px, so "on the hand" means "at the hand", not the hand's shape.
- **Not yet known:** whether hand points help find the paddle or a hit. That is the next test, if pursued.

## Paddle hits: "is a player's hand next to the ball?" works on the events we have

- When the ball changes direction, a hit has a hand right next to the ball and a bounce usually doesn't.
  On the 36 events the operator tagged (23 bounces, 13 hits) this separates them very well (AUC 0.95):
  passes the bar set in advance.
- The body model's wrist point works just as well (0.97), so the special hand model isn't needed for this.
- Ball speed (a hit speeds the ball up) and hand distance catch different mistakes, so they can be combined.
- **Not proven yet:** all 36 events come from one 3-minute stretch. The check on new events needs the
  operator to tag 28 events in CVAT project 5 (about 15 minutes). And we still don't know what share of all
  hits the system sees in the first place.
