# 09.30 progress overview

## Console Members tab now shows real players (ADR-145)

The Members tab used to be entirely made-up data. Now it shows real players who have checked in through the venue's QR code (the self-serve sign-in shipped 2026-09-29), with when they last played and how many reels they got this month. Two of the mockup's columns — Membership tier and Reel access — got dropped instead of faked, since there's no data behind either yet.

**Worth knowing:** this list only includes people who checked in themselves through the QR code. A booking made from the venue's own Schedule tab doesn't carry any player identity, so this isn't a full member list yet — just the self-serve ones. Checked directly against production (impersonating a real brand owner): a venue sees its own checked-in player correctly, and a stranger is correctly refused. Not yet watched through an actual signed-in browser session — production only has the operator's own 3 test accounts so far, no real venue-goer has used it.

Built, tested, and pushed to production later the same day (see the commit/push note at the bottom).

## Privacy policy caught contradicting itself, one line fixed (ADR-146)

Went back through the whole product against the three published legal pages, since the player system built this week and last was never checked against them. Found the privacy policy's own scope section said this site "does not directly serve, or build an account relationship with, end players" — true when written a few days ago, false since the self-serve LINE/Google/email check-in went live the next day. The player's consent checkbox on the check-in and signup pages links straight to that same page.

Fixed: the privacy policy now describes the self-serve player relationship plainly — what's collected (display name, email if not via LINE, which sessions, consent record with IP/user agent) and that the same access/deletion rights apply. Bumped the version string every consent record is stamped with, since a policy that changed materially shouldn't silently cover people who agreed to the old text — the operator's own 2 test consents now correctly show as not current and will be asked again.

A real email from a prospective venue (Pickle Day Social Club, replying to an outreach from a month ago) asked directly what terms exist for image use, authorization, and data breach — answering that honestly is what surfaced how thin the "data breach" piece actually was: nothing anywhere committed to telling anyone if a breach happened. Added one sentence to the same privacy policy section committing to notify affected people if that ever occurs — a documented commitment, not a built incident-response system, since that's what was actually missing.

Also added a one-line age/guardian self-declaration to the self-serve check-in and consent pages ("by checking in, you confirm you're 18+, or a guardian has agreed") — folded into the existing consent text, not a new checkbox or database column. Proportionate to a small pickleball app, same approach LINE/Google's own signup flows take.

While drafting the actual reply to Pickle Day, found a real live bug while double-checking the notice-template link the reply points to: a self-serve player's "Filming notice" link goes straight to a page that's a fill-in-the-blank template for venues — full of unfilled `[場館名稱]` brackets, never meant to be read directly. Restructured it: a new plain-language section up top now covers the same ground as the privacy policy (retention, training, breach notice, and the new self-serve QR path) in complete sentences with nothing to fill in, and the original copy-paste template stays below, now clearly labeled as being for venues.

Also went back and re-verified the cloud retention numbers directly against Cloudflare's own API rather than trusting a code comment — a real, enabled 90-day object lifecycle rule exists on the reels bucket. Every cloud-side retention promise is confirmed real. The local-machine gap below is the only one still open.

Reviewing the exact wording out loud with the operator caught one more thing: the AI-training clause said a future notice "should explain" de-identification measures — a spec for a document that doesn't exist, not actual content. Checked the pipeline directly: zero face-detection or blurring code anywhere, so there'd be nothing concrete to say even if asked. Operator's call: commit only to training data never being linked back to a player's name, account or contact info — already effectively true since no training pipeline exists, and doesn't promise engineering work that hasn't been built. Added as one identical sentence to both the notice template and the privacy policy.

**Not fixed yet, found in the same pass, worth doing next:**
- Local recordings on real venue machines still never get deleted — the fix (ADR-141) is written and tested but hasn't gone out in a desktop release yet.
- The privacy policy's list of who processes your data doesn't mention Supabase, Cloudflare, RunPod or ECPay — only four unrelated tools from before the real infrastructure existed.
- No venue is actually signed and paying yet (PGC is informal, billing price is still unset) — worth keeping in mind when replying to Pickle Day.

Everything above committed and pushed to production the same day, across all four repos (`pic-vision`, `pic-vision-cloud-console`, `pic-vision-website`, `pic-vision-reel`) — checked live afterward by curling the real pages, not just trusting the push succeeded. Along the way, merged `pic-vision-reel` against 4 unrelated commits the operator had pushed separately (vertical 9:16 share test page, top-10 rally score ordering, swipe-loaded videos, dropped Vercel analytics) with no conflicts.

## AI-training consent moved off the venue entirely, onto the player directly (ADR-147)

The PGC gap above (training flag on, no signed consent) led to a bigger call: a venue was never the right party to consent to a *player's* footage being used for training. Removed the venue's toggle and checkbox entirely — now it's a direct choice between PicVision and each player, a third, always-optional checkbox next to the two mandatory ones, wired through every way someone can sign up or consent (email, LINE, Google, and the standalone fallback page). PGC's flag is now off; with no venue-level basis left and no player having gone through the new mechanism yet, there was nothing left to justify treating its footage differently, so it goes back to the normal 14-day rule.

Caught a real, already-existing bug while touching this: `pic-vision-reel`'s own copy of the consent-version constant had stayed on the old date when the database side was bumped earlier today — the two were supposed to be hand-kept in sync and weren't, so the standalone `/consent` page would have quietly disagreed with the check-in flow about whether a player's consent was current. Fixed.

Also, while merging two disconnected consent lines into one sentence earlier today, made the same fix everywhere it renders, then further reworded the age statement and made the two legal links actually look clickable (they inherited the surrounding paragraph's colour, same as regular text) — a real operator on a real phone couldn't tell they were links. And separately: the app now defaults to Traditional Chinese instead of English whenever it can't tell what a visitor's phone wants (no Accept-Language match, or none sent at all, which some in-app browsers do) — every real venue and player here is in Taiwan, so English was the wrong default to fall back to.

Built, tested (`tsc`, full suites, `next build` clean across `pic-vision-cloud-console` and `pic-vision-reel`), verified directly against production via impersonation, and pushed the same day.
