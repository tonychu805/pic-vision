# 2026-08-23 — Pre-serve stillness signal tested against PIC-37's known false positives: does not generalize past brickwall

Picked up from 2026-08-22's stillness/position work. That session's own stated next step — never run until today — was to check whether the brickwall-derived stillness dip (real serves show near-team ankle speed dropping to 7–19% of the surrounding dead-time baseline) is actually absent at `PIC-37`'s already-known, unambiguous false positives on `IMG_7743` post-bump (all 12 confirmed by trajectory-plot read as real dead-time crossings, no ambiguity). If the fusion idea for `PIC-31` (stillness-dip gates a crossing burst) was going to hold up, this was the direct test.

## What was built

`scripts/pose_stillness.py` — a real, reusable version of yesterday's interactive scratch analysis (`yolov8n-pose` + ByteTrack, ankle-midpoint frame-to-frame speed, immediate-vs-baseline ratio). Closes the reproducibility gap flagged by yesterday's committee review. Reimplementation sanity-checked against brickwall's 3 known boundaries first (0.10/0.19/0.15 vs. the original 0.08/0.17/0.07 — same sharp-dip pattern) before trusting it on new data.

## What it found

The signal does **not** transfer to `IMG_7743`. Two results together make this a clear rejection, not just noise:

1. IMG_7743's own real rally starts (n=6) don't show the dip either — mean ratio 1.36, none under brickwall's 0.2 cutoff. A brickwall-calibrated filter would reject every real rally on this video.
2. IMG_7743's 12 confirmed dead-time false positives (n=11 usable) sit in the same range as IMG_7743's own real rallies — mean 0.94 vs. 1.36 — no separation, and if anything the wrong direction.

Full numbers: `EXPERIMENTS.md`, 2026-08-23 entry. Recorded as `DECISIONS.md` ADR-055 — the stillness ratio, in its brickwall-derived fixed-threshold form, is not adopted as a `PIC-31` signal. `PIC-31` stays open.

## Update, end of day — relabeled, re-scored, and the story flipped again

Logged to Linear: PIC-49 filed (relabel task, blocks PIC-31), comments added to PIC-37 and PIC-31 pointing at the retraction. Launched `label_web.py` over Tailscale for the actual relabel pass — the user did a full two-layer presence + quality pass over the whole postbump segment (not just the 12 known gaps), landing at **6 → 53 labels**, 5 of them `quality:1`.

Re-scored against the shipped default: **precision 0.818, recall 0.340.** But that's not the real headline — checking the 4 residual "false positives" against their nearest label, all 4 are boundary near-misses (IoU 0.31–0.47) on real rallies, not junk. **Precision is effectively perfect; there is no dead-time/false-positive problem left on this video at all.** `PIC-31`'s whole premise for IMG_7743 — a signal to separate real rallies from junk crossings — turns out to have very little left to solve here.

The real, newly-visible problem is recall, and it's mechanically explained: 30 of 35 misses have fewer than `min_crossings=6` raw crossings, so they never produced a segment at all — invisible to every prior false-positive analysis, PIC-37 included, for the same structural reason. A diagnostic sweep (not a parameter change — `IMG_7743` is locked `eval`) shows recall caps around 0.45 no matter how low `min_crossings` goes, while precision collapses — so it's not just a mistuned constant, some very short real rallies (1–2 net crossings) are below what crossing-count clustering can ever catch.

**Softens the urgency, though:** only 1 of 5 highlight-worthy (`quality:1`) rallies was missed — 4/5 caught. The 35 misses are almost all ordinary short exchanges. For the current highlight-reel phase, this matters less than the raw recall number suggests; it would matter more for the longer-term analytics vision.

The one missed `quality:1` rally traced to a genuine ~9s tracking gap mid-rally (zero crossings detected), not a threshold issue at all.

Recorded as `DECISIONS.md` ADR-059 (corrects ADR-058). `PIC-50` needs correcting — its premise no longer holds as scoped. General takeaway worth keeping: label honestly and exhaustively (that part was right), but *score* recall split by quality grade, not blended — a blended number across mostly-filler labels is actively misleading.

## Update, digging deeper on the stillness idea — does it mark the rally start? And a labeling-convention finding that reframes a lot of today

User asked whether the stillness dip actually identifies *when* a rally starts, not just whether one is coming. Ran a proper localization test on brickwall — 17 rally starts spread across the whole video (not just the opening minutes checked before): the dip shows up reliably (15/17, 88%), but its timing lands a median of ~1.07s *before* the labeled start, with real spread (std 1.3s). Only 29% land within the project's own ±0.5s boundary target.

Then the actual explanation came out: today's relabeling intentionally padded `start` a few seconds before the real serve, for viewer lead-in — "varies a lot, usually within 3 seconds," not consciously tracked for consistency. That's a real deviation from `LABELING.md`'s documented "serve contact" rule, and it's a very plausible full or partial explanation for the offset just measured — not signal imprecision, a labeling-convention artifact.

Asked the user how to resolve it: **codify the lead-in as the intentional rule** (recommended and chosen) rather than re-tightening to literal contact — a highlight reel benefiting from a few seconds of setup context is arguably correct product behavior. `LABELING.md` bumped to v4 with this documented. Recorded as `DECISIONS.md` ADR-062, which also flags the real, not-yet-resolved consequence: `eval/harness.py`'s IoU matching wasn't built for a boundary that intentionally moves 0-3s per rally, and this could be confounding some of today's earlier boundary-fragment characterization (ADR-060) as well as the stillness-localization numbers themselves.

**Not yet done:** decide how `eval/harness.py` should handle the intentional start lead-in (needs its own issue); re-run the stillness-localization and fragment checks once that's settled; the same localization test on IMG_7743, which showed no dip at all previously — worth checking whether that was also a labeling-convention artifact.

## Update — labels landed on brickwall-SEMI, and the picture changes: precision holds, recall doesn't, and it's not the story we expected

Full blind PRESENCE+GRADE label pass done (`eval/labels/brickwall-SEMI.jsonl`, 38 rallies). One process wrinkle: the labeler ran against the raw untrimmed source rather than the trimmed 0–900s clean segment, so 2 labels landed in the post-bump region the detector never scored — cleanly excluded, 36 used.

The operator's playback review of the 31 candidates (30/31 real) only ever checked precision. Formal scoring against the fresh labels (`TECH_SPEC.md` §11.2, IoU≥0.5 — the number comparable to the other three videos) tells a different story: **recall 63.9% (23/36), precision 74.2% (23/31).** Recall this low on a held-out video is new and worth taking seriously rather than explaining away.

Dug into why, playback-verified frame-by-frame at all 7 near-miss boundary cases (segments that exist but fall just under the IoU threshold) — not a stills-based rally/dead-time guess, but confirming an unambiguous "point over" signal (paddle-tap, players walking off) at each boundary. 6 of 7 show the detector's segment truncated inside a real, longer point — it stops recording crossings 3–4s before the point actually ends, and/or starts several seconds late. **First read was that this was a new detection-chain problem — wrong, corrected after checking the actual crossing-time data.** All 7 turn out to be the same already-documented `gap_sec=3.0`/`min_crossings=6` fragmentation mechanism as the "candidate 0" example found earlier today, just appearing on both ends of a point instead of only at the start: a real, legitimate crossing exists, it's just isolated from its neighbors by a hair over the 3.0s cutoff (two cases missed by exactly 0.03s) or in too small a group to reach `min_crossings=6` alone. One case (rally 1's tail) does have a genuine ~2.2s raw TrackNet detection void, but even that recovers a real crossing afterward that then gets dropped by the same clustering mechanism. Doesn't change the standing call not to raise `gap_sec` — if anything it's more evidence for why that knob doesn't generalize.
