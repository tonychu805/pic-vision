# 2026-10-26 progress overview

## Correction: the "PGC 60fps" footage was a downloaded match, not PGC

Yesterday's write-up leaned on footage I'd labeled "PGC 1080p@60." The operator caught that
those clips (`pgc-fieldtest-sept2026/*/sample-clip.mp4`) are **a pro pickleball match
downloaded off YouTube**, not the PGC camera. Verified it: the files carry a YouTube/ffmpeg
download signature and have no raw recording beside them. **The real PGC field-test footage is
the 30fps 1080p `.mkv` continuous recordings** — there is no 60fps anywhere in the actual
capture.

### What changes
- **The fps part of the story is downgraded.** "The pipeline throws away 60→30fps" isn't true
  for PGC — PGC *records* 30fps. The 60-vs-30 comparison was done by downsampling the
  downloaded 60fps clip, so the 60fps benefit only exists if a camera actually captures more
  than 30fps. That's a camera-configuration question, not a free pipeline flag.
- **"Far-tile generalizes to PGC 1080p" is retracted.** That test ran on the downloaded match.
  It's still a valid second data point on a different real source, but it doesn't speak to
  PGC's behind-baseline camera.
- Several labels ("9px native ball," "density 13–18%") were the downloaded match, now relabeled.

### What still holds
- The **resolution** finding: feeding a full 1920-wide frame into fixed-512 networks shrinks
  the far ball (~9px → ~2px) and the far player's pose below what the models resolve. This was
  also shown on **brickwall, a real camera**, and the math is general.
- The **far-tile fix** (zoom into the far court, then detect) recovers the far ball *and* the
  far swing → makes far-court hits capturable. Validated on brickwall (real) and the downloaded
  match. Soft/no-swing returns remain a floor.
- Keep TrackNet (the detector was never the problem).

### Honest status
The far-tile is validated on **one real camera (brickwall) plus a downloaded match** — **not on
the actual PGC production camera (30fps, behind-baseline).** That generalization is the real open
question. None of this is needed for the core reel.

## Later the same day: why clips miss the serve, and a fix that works (opt-in)

**What we found.** When a clip starts after the serve, it's usually because players at the kitchen hide the ball from
the camera during dinks. The system then thinks no shots crossed the net for a few seconds, decides the rally ended,
and throws the serve away. Confirmed by watching the clips, not just from numbers.

**What we tried that didn't work:** gluing earlier pieces back on, finding the serve from player arm swings, and
keeping rallies alive whenever the ball moves. Each made clips worse or merged separate points.

**What worked.** A narrow rule that only keeps a rally alive when the ball vanishes near the net and pops back up on the
far side, the "hidden on our side" pattern (ADR-160).
- On footage it wasn't designed on, 11 of 12 changed clips were better: 4 now catch the serve, and 6 are real points
  today's version drops.
- Brickwall precision went 0.70 → 0.86 and recall 0.63 → 0.73.

**Status.** Built behind a flag, off by default, with tests; the cloud reel path is unchanged.
- Known costs: an occasional toss-back tail at a clip's end, and one case of reaching back into a previous point.

**What's next.** The extra rallies mostly don't reach the reel's top 10, so ranking is now the limit on reel quality.
Turning the flag on in production needs a reel-level watch on new sessions first.
