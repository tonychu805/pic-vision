# Rally Detection Concepts

## What a rally is (LABELING.md v2)

A **rally** runs from **serve contact to the moment the ball becomes dead** — next touches the ground or goes out of play. "Ball-dead" is deliberately later than "last paddle contact" (up to ~1 s on balls sailing long, a large fraction of the 1.0 s boundary target). Edge cases decided once and frozen: serve faults and courtesy returns are never rallies; warm-up is excluded; interrupted rallies are labeled up to the interruption. Courtesy returns are *non-negotiable* non-rallies because they are the exact false positive the detector must reject — one happens after every point, so the error is systematic, not rare.

## The v1 signal: net crossings

The core idea (ADR-022, extended): a rally is a sequence of ball exchanges, and every exchange crosses the net. So **count the times the ball's image-y crosses the net line** and you have a rally signal that is strong exactly where player motion is weak (dink rallies — the case that killed v0).

Mechanics, all in `src/ball.py` (deliberately **backend-agnostic** — these functions accept any timed ball track: TrackNet, YOLO, or synthetic; this is what let ADR-046 swap detectors without touching the spine):

- **Image space, not court space.** The ball flies above the ground plane, so the calibration homography mis-projects it (parallax). From a behind-baseline camera, large image-y = near side, small image-y = far side. `net_line_y(calib)` gives the net's image-y: it **prefers the two hand-marked net-tape clicks** from `calibrate.py` and falls back to projecting court-y=22 through the homography (`net_image_y`) — **logging a warning on that fallback** (added 2026-08-16), because the derived line returns where the net's *base* meets the floor, never its tape (~130 px too low on IMG_7652, which has no `net_image_points`; every crossing count from such a calibration is biased, not noisy). The marked line exists because the derived line landed *below* the real net on zoomed footage (EXPERIMENTS 2026-08-10) — mark the **top of the tape**, since the ball crosses over it. When no calibration exists, `src/calib.py` resolves the line directly: interactive one-click picker (`pick_net_y`, the default) or headless Hough estimate (`detect_net_y`, rough) — priority order in ADR-041.
- **Hysteresis band.** `count_crossings` / `crossing_times` classify each frame near/far only when the ball is outside `net_y ± band`; frames inside the band or with no detection are skipped. This stops jitter near the net from inflating the count. The band encodes **court geometry, not detection quality** (ADR-042): during dink rallies the ball hovers within ±20 px of the net line, so band must be tuned per camera angle (expected 15–30 px at behind-baseline 1080p) even with a perfect detector. Tuning order: net_y → band → fine-tune model → recheck band.
- **Bursts → rallies.** `cluster_crossings(times, gap_sec, min_crossings)` groups crossings within `gap_sec` into segments and keeps only bursts with ≥ `min_crossings` — the courtesy-return suppressor (a tap-back produces exactly one crossing; a real rally several). Canonical defaults for the TrackNet path: `gap_sec=3.0, min_crossings=6` (**ADR-048** — tuned on the 33-label IMG_7743 benchmark with the `court_wedge` gate, precision 0.29 vs 0.12 at the old default of 3 for the same 61% recall; the earlier `min_crossings=3` was validated only on the IMG_7655 run under a since-superseded gate; `gap_sec=1.0` dissolves real rallies since crossings can be ~2 s apart).

## TrackNet — the active detector (ADR-046)

TrackNet is a 3-frame heatmap architecture: three consecutive frames → per-frame ball-position heatmap, thresholded at 0.5, largest blob wins. It sees the small, fast ball *across the net* where YOLO largely misses it — on the rally-#3 benchmark window it found **25 crossings vs YOLO's 5** (EXPERIMENTS 2026-08-12), even with domain-mismatched badminton weights. That 5× gap was too large to bridge with tuning, so the YOLO path was retired.

It is **CUDA-only** (TF Conv2D NCHW requires an NVIDIA GPU — the 2026-08-11 detector survey found it cannot run on macOS), so inference runs on a **RunPod GPU pod** (~$0.28/hr) **or the local RTX 2000 Ada workstation** (~58 fps, ~2× realtime — real-time inference is feasible there; EXPERIMENTS 2026-08-16. ADR-043's proxy trick — upload 720p, cut full-res locally — applies at venue scale):

- `scripts/pod_infer.py` runs **on the GPU box**: loads `TNV2_old_weights.h5` (badminton) or the pickleball fine-tuned `weights_k14_epoch19` with `compile=False` (Keras 3 refuses the legacy optimizer config; the k14 SavedModel loads under TF 2.15.1, the last Keras-2 release — EXPERIMENTS 2026-08-16), and emits `predictions.csv` with `Frame,Visibility,X,Y` at full frame rate, coordinates scaled to source resolution. It aborts below 98% of expected frames — a guard added after corrupt HEVC in IMG_7743/7744 made OpenCV silently stop decoding (930 of 121,013 frames, exit code 0); use the repaired `videos/*_fixed.mp4` sources.
- `src/tracknet.py` runs **locally**: `load_predictions` parses the CSV into a `(time_sec, x or None, y or None)` track (frame numbers ÷ fps; `Visibility=0` → `None`), then `rally_segments_from_predictions` gates and tracks that track before feeding the unchanged `crossing_times` → `cluster_crossings` spine. The full local chain is **court gate → `track_ball` → `crossing_times` → `cluster_crossings`** — the two pre-spine stages exist because TrackNet's per-frame best-guess ball can land on an *adjacent court's* real ball, and Y-only crossing detection can't tell (see the multi-court section below).
- `src/cut.py` glues it together (`cut_rallies_from_predictions`): segments get `score = crossings` (the exchange-count ranking from ADR-039), then `cut_clips` + `concat_clips` produce clips, `manifest.json`, and `highlight.mp4`. Full operator flow: [Key Workflows](../workflows/pipeline.md#5-run-detection-tracknet-on-gpu--the-active-path).

**Measured caveats (2026-08-16, EXPERIMENTS.md):** the pickleball k14 weights beat the badminton ones at strict IoU (11/36 vs 5/36 recall @ IoU≥0.5 on IMG_7655, boundary error 1.05 s vs 1.34 s) — domain-matched weights win on precision and boundary quality. **Do not mask the input frame before inference** — a hard mask edge is out-of-distribution and *cost* recall (0.52 vs 0.61); filter geometrically after inference instead. TrackNet's dead-time FP rate on the 659–666 s control window has only been run through the *YOLO+tracker* path, and the big one — **PIC-1: recall is pinned at 20/33 on IMG_7743 because the ball is barely detected during the 13 missed rallies** (a detection failure, not a gating one; undiagnosed — occlusion, motion blur, and ball-vs-floor contrast each imply a different fix).

## The retired YOLO detector (`archive/`, ADR-046)

The first ball detector ran locally: `detect_ball`/`detect_candidates` used off-the-shelf **yolov8x** on COCO class 32 (`sports ball`) at `imgsz=1280` (never 640 — ADR-006 measures ~60% → ~100% detection at 1280), with a `ball_box_ok` size/aspect filter (`max_ball_px` **mandatory**, ADR-045 — without it the class latches onto 30–60 px player heads/torsos instead of the 10–21 px ball) and the single-ball tracker below. Two facts killed it: yolov8x scores only 0.2–0.3 confidence **at the net** — *the crossing moment is intrinsically the hardest detection from a behind-baseline camera* — and TrackNet's 5× crossing count on identical footage. The code lives in `archive/yolo_detect.py` + `archive/yolo_pipeline.py` with its tests, recoverable if a local-inference path (fine-tuned yolov8n on Jetson, ADR-040) ever beats TrackNet on clean footage. `ball_box_ok` stays in `src/ball.py` as shared signal-side code. CoreML export (`yolov8x.mlpackage`, 1.7× on ANE, ADR-044) was the local-speed fix while the YOLO path was live.

## The single-ball tracker (`src/track.py`) — and the multi-court gates

The third failure mode found on real footage: in a multi-court gym a per-frame detector finds *real* balls that aren't in play (adjacent courts, idle balls). "Best ball anywhere per frame" hops between them, flipping net sides → phantom crossings during dead time. The standing regression evidence (YOLO path, CHECKLIST.md/PROGRESS.md): IMG_7652 659–666 s dead window produced **22 crossings without the tracker → 0 with it**, while the 58–77.5 s rally kept a strong 16.

`track_ball(frames, max_jump, reset_after=15)` follows one ball: initialize on the most confident candidate, then take the nearest candidate within `max_jump` px each frame; anything farther is a teleport (a different ball) and is rejected as a gap. After `reset_after` consecutive gaps the track releases so a new rally can re-acquire. It needs **dense per-frame candidates** so real ball motion stays under `max_jump` between frames. This is the "ball cannot teleport" max-step constraint arrived at independently by both prior-art projects (TECH_SPEC §2).

**The tracker is in the active TrackNet path** — `rally_segments_from_predictions` wraps each surviving detection as a single-candidate list and runs `track_ball` over it (`max_jump=150`, `reset_after=15`, the validated YOLO-path defaults) to catch within-court jitter and same-frame teleports. TrackNet emits one position per frame, but that position is a *best guess* that can sit on the wrong court, so the teleport check still earns its place.

**Why the tracker alone wasn't enough — the court gate (confirmed 2026-08-16 on IMG_7744).** TrackNet's per-frame best-guess ball can land on an adjacent court's real ball, whose natural up/down motion then crosses `net_y` and produces a phantom rally on an *empty* tracked court. `track_ball` does **not** fix this on real footage: its teleport-rejection lock releases after `reset_after` frames with no detection, and real dead time between rallies routinely exceeds that — by the time the adjacent-court ball reappears, the tracker has forgotten the near court's last position and happily re-acquires on it. The fix is a **gap-independent gate** applied *before* `track_ball` ever sees a detection — so there's no reset window to exploit. Two shapes exist:

- **Flat X-gate** — `court_x_range(calib, margin_px=50)` in `src/calib.py` derives the tracked court's image-x bounds from `calibrate.py`'s 12 clicked court points; `rally_segments_from_predictions`'s `court_x_min`/`court_x_max` drop anything outside. `src/cut.py` auto-derives the bounds from `--calib`, or takes explicit `--court-x-min/--court-x-max` (with `--court-margin`, default 50 px). Limitation: on a behind-baseline view the near corners span nearly the whole frame — on IMG_7743 it derived [4, 1915] on a 1920 px frame and excluded nothing.
- **Perspective `court_wedge`** — `court_wedge(calib, margin_px=160, cap_court_heights=0.7, spread=0.5)` returns an `(x, y) -> bool` predicate that follows the court's trapezoid taper with depth (the taper is strongly non-linear), widens above the far baseline by `spread` (a high ball hit near the camera is high in the image but still horizontally wide), and is **hard-capped ~0.7 court-heights above the far baseline**. The cap earns its keep twice: it drops ceiling/light-fixture false rallies, *and* it raises recall — those high spurious detections were hijacking `track_ball` mid-rally and costing real crossings. Passed as `in_court=` to `rally_segments_from_predictions`, it supersedes the flat interval. Measured on the 33-label IMG_7743 benchmark at `min_crossings=6`: **precision 0.29 at recall 0.61, vs 0.10 at the same recall for the flat interval** (EXPERIMENTS 2026-08-16, "capped trapezoid gate works"). Caveat: it is **API-only today** — `src/cut.py`'s CLI wires the flat interval, not the wedge. Known gap: the cap is a fraction of the court's *image* height, so on a higher mount it can land off-frame and silently disable itself (it does on IMG_7652); redefining it in real ball height — the marked net tape is a free ruler (~100 px/m on 7743) — is the recorded follow-up.

The regression tests are `test_rally_segments_rejects_adjacent_court_teleport` (same-frame hop), `test_rally_segments_court_gate_survives_reset_after_gap` (the reset-exploit case), and the three `test_court_wedge_*` tests (taper rejection, airspace kept, ceiling cap) in `tests/test_tracknet.py`.

## Court calibration (`calibrate.py`)

Manual, once per camera mount (ADR-007 — three prior-art projects independently concluded hand-clicking beats trained keypoint regressors on unfamiliar angles). The operator clicks **12 court intersections in any order** (order-independent assignment, ADR-035 — a real 28.2 ft mis-ordered calibration recovered to 0.36 ft) **plus 2 net-tape points**, onto a canonical 20 × 44 ft court (net at y=22, NVZ lines at y=15/29). Output JSON: homography, per-point and RMSE reprojection error (feet), and `net_image_points`. `src/players.py` consumes the homography (`load_calibration`, `to_court`); `src/ball.py` consumes the net points.

## v0 player markers (frozen baseline)

`src/players.py` (YOLO person detection → foot point → court feet → asymmetric on-court filter: tight ±4 ft sidelines, generous ±10 ft baselines for lob retrieval) and `src/events.py` (`mean_motion` — tracking-free nearest-neighbour displacement; `n_at_kitchen` — players within 4 ft of an NVZ line, the dink-formation signal that is live even at near-zero motion). These implement the ADR-037 two-sided marker idea. They measured **inverted** on casual play (2026-08-08) — dead time more active than low-energy rallies — which is why v1 is primary, but v0 was never fairly tested (compromised footage), so it stays frozen per ADR-039.

## Known failure modes (all measured, EXPERIMENTS.md)

1. **Zoom/pan invalidates everything** — the net line moved y=260 → 170 between two rallies of one clip. Fixed mount is non-negotiable; one calibration cannot hold across a zooming clip.
2. **Derived net line ≠ real net** on any calibration made from a differently-framed shot → mark the net directly (shipped).
3. **Small far ball at the net** — confidence cutoff alone can't catch crossings. YOLO's answer (size filter + tracker) measured insufficient → TrackNet; a higher/side-on mount remains a capture lever (ADR-038: prove software before hardware).
4. **Out-of-play balls** — the YOLO path fixed this with the tracker; the TrackNet path needed more, because the tracker resets during real dead time and re-acquires on an adjacent court's ball. Fixed (2026-08-16) by the **court gate** (above) + `track_ball` for within-court teleports. The remaining FP control still owed is a dead-window (659–666 s) run through the gated TrackNet path.
5. **Dink rallies vs. dead time** — indistinguishable by player motion; the reason the ball signal exists.
6. **Ball simply not detected during real rallies (PIC-1)** — 13 of 33 IMG_7743 rallies are missed under every gate shape and threshold tried because the ball barely appears in `predictions.csv` during them. Undiagnosed (occlusion / motion blur / contrast each imply a different fix); the recorded next step is to render those windows with detections drawn on and look.
